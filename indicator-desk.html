1. Four tabs for every indicator question

Tab Page What it answers What I copy from it
1 SDG Indicators Metadata Repository What the number counts and where it stops §2.a definition, §2.b unit, §4.b limits, §0.g custodian, the Last updated date
2 SDG Global Database The value Series code, country, year, value, the Nature flag, the source field
3 SDG API The same rows, for a script or for Claude The same fields, by series code and area code
4 SDG Data Contacts Who to ask when the metadata stops The focal point and agency for the indicator

The repository page says the metadata set was downloadable "as of 1 October 2026" when I opened it. Data Contacts says it was last updated 22 September 2026. Custodian agencies nominated a focal point for each indicator "concerning questions on definitions, methods of computation, data or other issues".

Worked example: 7.1.1, access to electricity

Metadata 7.1.1, 2026-03-27 edition. Custodian: World Bank Group (§0.g).

Section What it says
§2.a "Proportion of population with access to electricity is the percentage of population with access to electricity."
§3.a "Also, for the sake of consistency in methodology across countries, government and utility data are not considered."
§4.b The World Bank estimates demand side access rates. "This is different from the supply side access rates usually provided by governments, ministries, etc."
§4.c Survey values are reported in their original form. The model fills only the missing years

So a ministry connection rate and the 7.1.1 figure can both be right and still differ. The metadata says which one the SDG series holds.

2. Two rates, two bases

Both figures are in the Secretary-General's 2026 SDG progress report.

Indicator Report line Page
3.2.1 "The under -5 mortality rate was 37.4 deaths per 1,000 live births" in 2024 8
3.3.3 Malaria incidence rose "from 59 to 64 cases per 1,000 population at risk" between 2015 and 2024 9

Malaria: the title drops the base

Metadata 3.3.3, 2026-03-27 edition. Custodian: WHO, Malaria and Neglected Tropical Diseases Department (§0.g).

Section Text
§0.c Indicator "Indicator 3.3.3: Malaria incidence per 1,000 population"
§0.d Series "SH_STA_MALR - Malaria incidence per 1,000 population at risk [3.3.3]"
§2.a Definition "Incidence of malaria is defined as the number of new cases of malaria per 1,000 people at risk each year."
§2.b Unit "Cases per 1000 population at risk."

The population at risk is the UN population for the year times the proportion at risk estimated by the national malaria programme at baseline. The same proportion is used for the entire time series (§4.c). The metadata also says the estimated incidence can differ from the incidence a Ministry of Health reports, for example when reporting is incomplete or private facilities are left out (§4.b).

I tested the drafting step. I gave Claude the figure with the base left out, asked 2026-10-21 02:45 UTC: "The UN Secretary-General's 2026 SDG progress report puts global malaria incidence in 2024 at 64 per 1,000. Write one sentence for a briefing note that states this figure with its unit in full."

It wrote: "In 2024, global malaria incidence was 64 cases per 1,000 population at risk, according to the UN Secretary-General's 2026 SDG progress report." The base was right. The risk sits with whoever copies the indicator title instead of §2.b.

Under-5 mortality: a probability

Metadata 3.2.1, 2026-03-27 edition. Custodian: UNICEF (§0.g).

"The under-five mortality rate as defined here is, strictly speaking, not a rate (i.e. the number of deaths divided by the number of population at risk during a certain period of time), but a probability of death derived from a life table and expressed as a rate per 1,000 live births." (§2.a)

Asked the same day whether 37.4 per 1,000 live births can be written as 37.4 of every 1,000 children under five dying in 2024, Claude answered "Not quite" and said the share of all children under five who died that year "would be a lower figure".

The base check

Code Per Divided by Where it is written
3.3.3 1,000 population at risk §2.b, not the title
3.2.1 1,000 live births, as a probability §2.a
3.7.2 1,000 females in the respective age group §2.a, 2025-03-28 edition
7.1.1 percent population §2.a

Project instructions

Paste into Claude
Before any rate goes in my draft, check its base.

1 Open the metadata. Copy the unit in 2.b word for word.
2 Compare it with the indicator title in 0.c.
  Shorter title? Use 2.b and say so.
3 Write the base in full in the sentence:
  64 cases per 1,000 population at risk.
4 Two bases in one table: write BASE MISMATCH, name rows.

Never convert one base into another.
Under-5 mortality is a probability per 1,000 live births.
Do not write it as a share of children under five.

3. Spec the dashboard before the first tile

The named example: Rwanda, urban

SDG Global Database, EG_ACS_ELEC, Rwanda. Source field: World Bank.

Year Urban Nature Total Nature
2021 98.0 percent M 48.7 percent M
2022 98.0 percent M 50.6 percent M
2023 99.9 percent M 63.9 percent M
2024 88.0 percent C 72.0 percent C

Rural for 2024 is 65.2 percent and modelled. A trend tile draws the urban line as a fall of almost twelve points in one year. The first review question writes itself, and the answer is in the metadata, not in the chart: "results based on real survey data are reported in their original form for all years available. The statistical model is used to fill in data only for years where they are missing" (7.1.1 §4.c). The 2024 values flagged C are survey values. The years before them are model estimates.

I do not know from the database alone why the survey sits below the model. That is a question for the 7.1.1 focal point at the World Bank, listed in SDG Data Contacts.

The rule the database follows

From the metadata repository page: "Please note that no data are published in the Global SDG Indicators Database until the corresponding metadata is approved by the IAEG-SDGs." The database waits for the metadata. A dashboard can wait too.

4. Climate M&E in four layers

Layer Question it answers Open
1 UNFCCC reporting What must the country report, and how often The transparency framework and its MPGs, decision 18/CMA.1
2 SDG 13.2.2 Which emissions figure the world sees Metadata 13.2.2 and the SDG Global Database
3 PCCB Where the capacity gaps are The PCCB toolkit to assess capacity gaps and needs
4 UN CC:Learn Where the team gets trained UN CC:Learn courses

Layers 1 and 2 meet in one file

Metadata 13.2.2, 2025-12-12 edition. Custodian: United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC Secretariat) (§0.g).

  • "The BTRs are submitted every two years and include information on GHG emissions, policies and measures, progress towards targets, climate change impacts and adaptation, levels of support and capacity-building needs." (§2.a)
  • "Data is limited to Parties that submit their BTRs and CRTs." Developing country Parties have flexibility in scope and level of detail, so their information may be less comprehensive (§4.b)
  • "Data available for developing country Parties are usually only for selected years." (§5)

So the capacity-building needs a country states are in its own BTR. Start the assessment there.

Layer 3: two bodies called PCCB

UNSD runs the HLG-PCCB. The UN Statistical Commission created it on 6 March 2015 as the High-level Group for Partnership, Coordination and Capacity-Building for statistics for the 2030 Agenda. It is about statistics, not climate. The climate body is the Paris Committee on Capacity-building, under the UNFCCC, and its toolkit is the one for capacity gaps and needs under the Paris Agreement. Search "PCCB" and you can land on either.

Layer 4: UN CC:Learn

UN CC:Learn is "a collaborative initiative of 36 multilateral organizations working together to help countries build the knowledge and skills they need to take action on climate change". The secretariat is UNITAR.

What Claude said with no tools

Asked on 2026-10-21 at 02:45 UTC which official sources to open first for this assessment, Claude listed the MPGs, the NDC Registry, BTR submissions, review reports, CBIT and PCCB materials and finance portals, and ended with: "Check the dates: Citations and portal structures change, so confirm them on the UNFCCC site before you rely on them." That is a list to check, not a source map.

Project instructions

Paste into Claude
I am starting a capacity gap assessment for climate
transparency reporting. Work in four layers, in order.

1 Rules     what the BTR must contain (MPGs, 18/CMA.1)
2 Public    what SDG 13.2.2 holds, for which years
3 Gaps      the PCCB toolkit on capacity gaps and needs
4 Training  UN CC:Learn courses that match each gap

For each gap: the layer, the document and the section.
A gap with no document behind it: UNSOURCED.
PCCB here is the UNFCCC Paris Committee, not HLG-PCCB.
