Reusing text from a partner document marked Confidential

A check for one draft against two clauses of ST/SGB/2007/6, with the answers Claude gave with and without the skill.

The work moment

A partner sends a proposal stamped Confidential. The team wants to reuse its text in a public donor update. Two clauses of ST/SGB/2007/6, Information sensitivity, classification and handling, cover this.

The marking clause, [p 2] Section 3:

"3.2 Where information from an external source contains prior sensitivity markings, it shall retain those markings or shall be assigned a classification that provides a degree of protectio n greater than or equal to that of the entity that furnished the information."

The declassification clause, [p 3] Section 4:

"4.4 When declassifying information received from an outside source, the Organization shall give due regard to expect ations of confidentiality of that outside source and, if appropriate, shall seek the prior consent of the outside source."

What the test showed

The same question went to Claude twice on 2026-10-30, with the same tools. The draft line came almost word for word from the partner's proposal, and the update was for a public audience.

Without the skill, Claude said:

"This is from my memory of the policy, so check the current text before you rely on it."

On who can lift the marking, it said:

"Only the originator can lift it. Declassification is done by, or with the agreement of, whoever originated the information. Here that's the partner NGO."

With the skill, Claude opened the bulletin and quoted it:

"Where information from an external source contains prior sensitivity markings, it shall retain those markings or shall be assigned a classification that provides a degree of protection greater than or equal to that of the entity that furnished the information."

"When declassifying information received from an outside source, the Organization shall give due regard to expectations of confidentiality of that outside source and, if appropriate, shall seek the prior consent of the outside source."

It named the step the team was about to take:

"Putting it in a public update releases the partner's text without its marking. Under the bulletin, that is declassification."

It gave a recommendation and left the decision with the team:

"You haven't supplied any consent reply. Deciding whether asking is "appropriate" is your team's call, but here the recommendation is to ask: you're taking their wording almost word for word to a public audience."

It marked the gaps:

"Who in your office may sign off on the declassification, and how long the partner's marking lasts: NOT IN SOURCE (outside clauses 3.2 and 4.4)."

About the partner agreement, the answer without the skill said:

"your partnership or grant agreement with this NGO may add requirements. Agreements often include a confidentiality clause covering proposals."

The answer with the skill tied it to the bulletin:

"Clause 6.1 says the bulletin does not apply where a "legal undertaking[s] made by the Organization to third parties" covers the records. If you have a signed agreement with this partner that has its own confidentiality terms, check it as well."

About who the rule binds, the answer with the skill said:

"This bulletin governs the UN Secretariat. If your organization isn't part of the Secretariat, it's a guide to good practice rather than a binding rule."

The steps of the skill

The skill is called external-marking-check. It takes one draft and gives back one check. It runs these steps in order.

  • Open the bulletin. Find both clauses and check that the wording matches the quotes. If it does not open, say so and stop before any ruling.
  • Read the draft. List every labelled partner passage. If it is unclear whether a passage comes from the partner, ask the user.
  • Note the markings. For each passage, note the partner's marking and the marking the draft carries.
  • Apply the marking clause. A passage passes only if the draft keeps the partner's marking or carries a classification that protects at least as much. A public draft carries no protection. Partner text marked confidential in a public draft fails.
  • Apply the declassification clause. If the team wants to lower the marking or publish, say what the partner is likely to expect, based only on its marking and any reply supplied. Say whether consent should be sought first. Quote any consent reply.
  • Give a fix. For each failed passage, remove it, hold it until the partner agrees, or replace it with text the team owns that holds nothing from the partner's document.

If the bulletin does not open, the check writes SOURCE NOT OPENED. If the question goes beyond the two clauses, it writes NOT IN SOURCE.

Copy-paste prompt

Paste into Claude
Check my draft below for text taken from a partner document marked <marking as printed>. The draft is for <internal, donors only, or public>. Use only the UN Secretary-General's bulletin at https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=ST/SGB/2007/6&l=en&t=pdf and my draft. Quote the clause on external information with prior sensitivity markings and the clause on declassifying information from an outside source. For each labelled partner passage, say whether my draft keeps the partner's marking or one that protects at least as much, and whether I should seek the partner's consent before this goes out. Suggest a fix for any passage that fails. If you cannot open the bulletin, say so and do not answer from memory. I have left out the partner's confidential text and put a label where each passage sits.

Before you paste

Use your own working notes, leave out anything marked confidential, and use the AI tool your organization allows.
