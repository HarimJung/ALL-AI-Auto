# BTR Checklist

One country situation in, one checklist out. Each line names the decision and paragraph it comes from. A date that no opened document states is marked unverified.

**Inputs**
- `country`: optional. Matters only for whether it is a least developed country (LDC) or small island developing State (SIDS), and whether it is a developing country Party applying flexibility.
- `report`: which BTR, e.g. the second.

## Why this exists

BTR questions get answered from slides and blog posts that mix the old biennial update reports with the new framework, and that state deadlines without saying where they come from. A deadline in a brief needs a decision number and a paragraph, or it needs the word unverified next to it.

## Sources (use these and nothing else)

| Document | What it holds | File |
|---|---|---|
| Decision 1/CP.21, FCCC/CP/2015/10/Add.1 | Para 90, how often Parties report under Article 13 | `https://unfccc.int/resource/docs/2015/cop21/eng/10a01.pdf` |
| Decision 18/CMA.1 and its annex (the MPGs), FCCC/PA/CMA/2018/3/Add.2 | Decision para 3 (first BTR date), para 4 (LDC and SIDS discretion). MPG paras 5 and 6 (flexibility), para 10 (what a BTR contains), paras 11 to 16 (format and submission), para 58 (latest inventory year), para 198 (late submission) | `https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/CMA2018_03a02E.pdf` |
| UNFCCC page for the report in question | The secretariat's statement of the due date and the list of submissions | `https://unfccc.int/second-biennial-transparency-reports` (for BTR2) |
| Paris Agreement, Article 13 | The paragraphs 7 to 10 the decisions refer to. The Agreement is the annex to decision 1/CP.21 | Same file as decision 1/CP.21 |

The UNFCCC site often serves a bot check instead of the page. The PDFs usually open with a plain download. If a page does not open, say so and do not fill it from memory or a search summary.

## Steps

1. Open both decision PDFs. Copy each document symbol and its date from the first page.
2. Find the due date. Quote decision 18/CMA.1 para 3 for the first BTR and decision 1/CP.21 para 90 for the frequency.
3. For a later BTR, open the UNFCCC page for that report and quote its date. If the page does not open, write the date as derived from the two paragraphs above and mark it `UNVERIFIED: not stated in an opened document`.
4. Build the checklist from MPG para 10, one line per sub-paragraph, keeping the decision's own verb. "Shall" is required. "Should" is expected. Do not turn a "should" into a must.
5. Add the format and submission lines from MPG paras 12, 15 and 16, the inventory year rule from para 58, and the LDC and SIDS line from decision para 4 and MPG para 11.
6. If the country is a developing country Party, add the flexibility rule from MPG para 6 in one line.

## Rules

- **A date with no paragraph behind it is `UNVERIFIED`. Never give a deadline from memory, a news item or a search result summary as confirmed.**
- Quote the decision's words for every item. Shorten only by cutting, never by paraphrase that changes "shall" or "should".
- Keep BTRs apart from national communications and from biennial update reports. They are different reports under different decisions.
- If the question asks about a tool, a template or a table the opened documents do not describe, write `NOT IN SOURCE` and name the document that would hold it, if known.
- Never invent a number of tables, pages or chapters.

# Claim Check

A draft and its sources in, one row per sentence out. A sentence passes only when a line in the source says the same thing on the page the draft cites.

**Inputs**
- `draft`: up to about 15 sentences. Number them if they are not numbered.
- `sources`: the documents the draft cites, as links or files. Official UN documents only (see below).

## Why this exists

A reviewer checks the number and the page. A checker that reads from memory, or matches only the numbers, passes a sentence whose figure sits on another page or belongs to another sentence. Long UN reports repeat the same percentages for different subjects, so a number found on the right page is not yet a match.

## Sources (use these and nothing else)

- The documents the user gives, if they are published by the UN system: documents with a UN symbol (A/..., E/..., FCCC/...) from `documents.un.org` or the publishing office, and reports on `un.org`, `unstats.un.org` or a UN agency's own site.
- Nothing else. Do not confirm a sentence from memory, from a news story or from a different edition of the same report.
- A source the user names but you cannot open is not a source. Every sentence that cites it gets `NOT IN SOURCES` with the note "could not open".

## Steps

1. Open each source. Copy its symbol or title and its date as printed on the first page.
2. Work out how the document numbers its pages. Use the page number printed on the page. Say so if it differs from the PDF page count.
3. For each sentence, go to the page or section it cites. Look for a line that states the same claim: the same figure, the same year, the same unit and the same subject.
4. If the cited page has no such line, search the whole source. If the line is on another page, the verdict is `WRONG PAGE` and the row gives the right page.
5. If no page in any source has the line, the verdict is `NOT IN SOURCES`. Quote the closest line, if there is one, so the reader sees what the source does say.
6. Fill one row per sentence. Never merge two sentences into one row.

## Verdicts

| Verdict | Means |
|---|---|
| SUPPORTED | A line on the cited page states the same figure, year, unit and subject |
| WRONG PAGE | The line exists in the source but not on the page the draft cites |
| NOT IN SOURCES | No line in any source states it. This includes a figure that differs from the source by any amount |

A number that matches but belongs to a different subject is not support. A share of adults with a bank account does not support the same share of people with electricity.

## Answer layout

```
Source: <symbol or title>, <date as printed> · <URL> · read <today's date>
Pages: <printed page numbers | PDF page numbers>

| # | Sentence | Source | Page/section | Quoted line | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | <first words of the sentence> | <symbol> | p. <n>, para <n> | "<line, word for word>" | SUPPORTED |
| 2 | ... | ... | cited p. <n>, found p. <m> | "<line>" | WRONG PAGE |
| 3 | ... | ... | cited p. <n> | closest: "<line>" or none | NOT IN SOURCES |

<n> of <total> supported.
```

## Rules

- **Cannot open a source: say which one and mark its sentences NOT IN SOURCES with "could not open". Do not judge them from memory.**
- Quotes are word for word. If text extraction breaks spacing, fix only the spacing.
- Never round, convert or recompute a figure to make it match. A source that says 12% does not support 13%.
- Do not rewrite the draft. The table says what is wrong, the author fixes it.
- Do not add sources the draft did not cite. If a better source exists, say so in one line under the table.

## Copy-paste prompt (works in any assistant)

> Check my draft against its sources, one sentence at a time. For each sentence, open the source and the page it cites, and give me a table: sentence, source, page or section, the line from the source word for word, and a verdict. The verdict is SUPPORTED only if a line on the cited page states the same figure, year and subject. If the line is on another page, write WRONG PAGE and give the right page. If no line in the source says it, write NOT IN SOURCES and quote the closest line. Do not judge any sentence from memory. If you can't open a source, say so for every sentence that cites it.

# Classified Intake Steps

One classified item in, one intake card out. Each step quotes the bulletin. Anything the opened text does not state is marked NOT IN SOURCE.

**Inputs**
- `document`: the user's own working notes about the item. This can be the cover email with the attachment removed, or a short note of what arrived and how it is marked. It is never the classified content itself.
- `question`: what the user wants to do with the item, such as get a summary or pass it to a colleague.

## Why this exists

A classified attachment looks like any other file in an inbox. The quick move is to drop it into an AI tool for a summary. The bulletin asks for a paper step on receipt and protected systems for any electronic handling. Unless your office has approved an AI tool for classified work, treat it as an unprotected system. Staff need the steps at the moment the file arrives, with the bulletin's own words behind each one.

## Sources (use these and nothing else)

| Document | What it holds | File |
|---|---|---|
| Secretary-General's bulletin on classified information | The paragraph on printing items received in electronic form, the paragraph on filing and storage it points to, the paragraph on protected means of communication, and the paragraph on controls for automated information systems | `https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=ST/SGB/2007/6&l=en&t=pdf` |
| The user's own document | What arrived, how it is marked, and what the user wants to do | Pasted or attached by the user |

The UN documents site sometimes returns an error or a bot check instead of the PDF. If the bulletin does not open, say so plainly. Do not fill the steps from memory or a search summary. Give the user the link and stop.

## Steps

- Read the user's document first. If it holds classified content, stop. Tell the user to remove it from the chat and not to paste it again. Work only from the marking and the user's own notes.
- Open the bulletin PDF. Copy the document symbol and the title from the first page.
- Find the paragraph that begins "(e) A hard copy of classified information received in an electronic form". Quote it as the receipt step.
- Find the subparagraph that the receipt step points to with the words "as detailed in subparagraph (d) above". Quote what it says on filing and storage. If the text is not there, write NOT IN SOURCE.
- Find the paragraph that begins "(f) Electronic transmission". Quote it as the sending step.
- Find the paragraph that begins "Heads of departments and offices". Quote the part on "controls that both prevent access by unauthorized persons".
- Answer the user's question. If they asked AI to summarise, read, translate or rewrite the classified item, say no and quote the sending step. Then list what AI can still help with on the unclassified side. Examples are a reply to the sender asking which protected channel to use, a plan for the user's own unclassified tasks, or a checklist built from this card.
- Say who decides which systems are protected. The bulletin gives this to heads of departments and offices working with IT services. Tell the user to ask their office which systems are approved. Do not name a system yourself.

- If the question asks about a classification level, a form or a procedure the opened text does not describe, write NOT IN SOURCE and point the user to their office.

# Country climate card

One input: a country name. One output: a card a delegation adviser can read in ten seconds before a bilateral.
Every number comes from an API response you saved. Nothing comes from memory.

## 1. Pull the data (save each response to a file before you read it)

Use the ISO3 code (Kenya = `KEN`) and the capital's latitude and longitude to two decimals.

| What | URL |
|---|---|
| Daily max temperature, capital | `https://archive-api.open-meteo.com/v1/archive?latitude=<lat>&longitude=<lon>&start_date=1961-01-01&end_date=2025-12-31&daily=temperature_2m_max&timezone=auto` |
| Total emissions, one call per year 2015 to 2024 | `https://api.climatetrace.org/v6/country/emissions?countries=<ISO3>&since=<year>&to=<year>` |
| Emissions by sector, 2024, one call per sector | same URL with `&since=2024&to=2024&sectors=<sector>` for `agriculture buildings fluorinated-gases forestry-and-land-use fossil-fuel-operations manufacturing mineral-extraction power transportation waste` |
| NDC content | `https://www.climatewatchdata.org/api/v1/data/ndc_content?countries[]=<ISO3>&per_page=500` |

Climate TRACE: read `[0].emissions.co2e_100yr` (tonnes). `since` and `to` must be the same year, or you get a sum of years.
Open-Meteo is CC BY 4.0: credit it on the card.

## 2. Compute in code (Python, standard library is enough)

- **Hot-day line** `T` = the 90th percentile of all daily maxima 1961–1990, rounded to a whole degree.
- **Hot days per year** = days with max ≥ `T`. `base` = mean 1961–1990, `recent` = mean 2016–2025, `ratio` = recent / base.
- **Emissions**: total 2024 and change since 2015, in Mt CO₂e.
- **Sector shares**: drop `forestry-and-land-use` (it is often a net sink and swamps the rest), then each sector's share of the sum of the others. `top` = largest.
- **NDC**: for `ghg_target`, `conditionality` and `submission`, take the row with the highest `id` (the latest submission).
- **The sentence** (same rule for every country):
  - power share < 10% and top is not power → «Power is not its problem. Its <top> are.» (a small power share is not a clean grid, so never write «clean»)
  - otherwise → «Its <top> lead, at <share>%. <Second> come next, at <share>%.»
  - nouns: agriculture farms, transportation roads, manufacturing factories, buildings buildings, waste landfills, fossil-fuel-operations oil and gas fields, mineral-extraction mines, fluorinated-gases cooling gases.

## 3. Lay out the card (4:5, 1080 × 1350)

Top to bottom: the prompt line · country name · **hero `<ratio>×` more hot days in <capital>** with base and recent under it ·
bar chart of hot days per year 1961–2025, 2016–2025 in a warm colour, flat lines at base and recent · the sentence, second line bold ·
three tiles (emissions 2024 · biggest source · latest NDC) · one source line.
Phone rule: nothing a reader must read under 24 px.

## City mode («heat card for <city>»)

Open-Meteo only, with `daily=temperature_2m_max,temperature_2m_min` at the city's coordinates. Same hot-day line `T`.
Hero = `recent − base` more hot days a year. Add warm nights: nights with minimum above 20 °C, 1961–90 mean against 2016–25 mean.
Tiles: the hot-day line `T`, the worst year (most hot days), the hottest day in the series.

## 4. Hand back

The card, then a JSON block with `T base recent ratio total24 growth top share_top power_share ndc_submission conditionality sentence`,
then the raw `ghg_target` sentence word for word.

## What a person still checks

- The NDC line against the country's own NDC document on unfccc.int. Climate Watch is a reading of it.
- The grid cell: ERA5 is about 25 km. A coastal capital can land on sea.
- Emissions are Climate TRACE estimates, not the national inventory. Say so if the card goes into an official brief.

# Declassification Sequence Check

You give it one transfer plan and get back one sequence card, with each step tied to a quoted line of the bulletin.

**Inputs**
- `document`: required. The user's own transfer plan, checklist, email or file list. Working notes are fine.
- `markings`: optional. The classification levels the files carry, in the user's own words.

## Why this exists

Offices close a project, pack the boxes and send them to the Archives. Often nobody thinks of declassification review until the boxes have gone. The bulletin sets the order. Review comes first. Transfer to the Archives comes after. A plan with these steps the wrong way round needs to be caught before the boxes move.

## Sources (use these and nothing else)

| Document | What it holds | File |
|---|---|---|
| Secretary-General's bulletin on information sensitivity, classification and handling | The declassification section. The paragraph on marking a date or event that triggers declassification. The paragraph on review before transfer to the Archives. The sub-paragraph on item-by-item review of strictly confidential records | `https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?s=ST/SGB/2007/6&l=en&t=pdf` |
| The user's own document | The steps the office plans to take, in its own order | Pasted or attached by the user |

If the bulletin does not open, say so in the first line of the answer. Do not fill in its wording from memory or from a search summary. Check the user's document for order only, and mark every bulletin line UNVERIFIED.

## Steps

- Open the bulletin. Copy its symbol and title from the first page.
- Find the declassification section. Copy the paragraph that begins "4.1 The originator of the information".
- Copy the paragraph that begins "4.3 Review for possible declassification shall take place before records are transferred".
- If the user's document mentions strictly confidential files, copy the sub-paragraph that begins "(a) Records that are classified as “strictly confidential”".
- Read the user's document. List each step in the order it appears. Keep the user's words.
- Put each step into one of three stages. Set a declassification date or event. Review the records. Transfer them to the Archives and Records Management Section. Steps that fit none of these go under Other.
- Compare the user's order with the bulletin's order. Flag any transfer that comes before review. Flag any classified file that has no date or event marked on it. Keep the bulletin's words "where appropriate" next to that flag.
- Write the sequence card.

## Answer layout

```
Sequence check · bulletin read <today's date>   (or: bulletin did not open, every quote UNVERIFIED)

Your order:     <step> / <step> / <step>
Bulletin order:
  Set:       "<quote from the marking paragraph>"
  Review:    "<quote from the review before transfer paragraph>"
  Transfer:  to the Archives and Records Management Section, after review

| Your step | Stage | In order? | Bulletin line |
|---|---|---|---|
| <user's words> | review | no, it comes after transfer | "<quote>" |

Strictly confidential: "<quote from sub-paragraph (a)>"   (only if the document mentions it)
Fix: <which step to move, in one line>
Not covered: <anything asked that the bulletin does not state>
```

## Rules

- **Review comes before transfer. Quote the bulletin for this every time. Never state the order from memory.**
- Quote the bulletin's own words. Shorten only by cutting. Keep "shall" and "where appropriate" exactly as written.
- Work only on the user's document. Do not add steps it does not mention, apart from the three stages.
- Do not name who in the user's office may carry out the review. Quote what the bulletin says about who reviews and stop there.
- How to recall boxes that have already gone is NOT IN SOURCE. Say so. Name the Archives and Records Management Section as the office that holds them.
- Do not work out when a record becomes old enough for review. Quote the sub-paragraph and leave the counting to the user.
- Use your own working notes, leave out anything marked confidential, and use the AI tool your organization allows.

**Inputs**

**Inputs**

## Steps

1. Open the metadata PDF. Copy the **last updated** date from the page header.
2. Copy the custodian agencies from **§0.g** and the first sentence of the definition from **§2.a**, word for word.
3. Read **§4.c** (computation), **§4.f** (missing values) and **§4.g** (aggregation). Pick the one rule a reader is most likely to get wrong. Quote it word for word with its §.
